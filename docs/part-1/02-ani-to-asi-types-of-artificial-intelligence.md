### Based on capability

<FoundGrid>
<div>
<h3>01 · Narrow AI</h3>
Built for a <Highlight>bounded set of tasks</Highlight>. It can be extremely capable inside that boundary.

**Today:** spam filters, recommenders, and current chat assistants.
</div>
<div>
<h3>02 · Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)</h3>
Would <Highlight>transfer learning across unfamiliar domains</Highlight>, plan over long horizons, and stay reliable without a new model for every job.

**Status:** disputed research goal, not an established achievement.
</div>
<div>
<h3>03 · Superintelligence</h3>
A hypothetical system that would exceed the best human ability across nearly every important cognitive domain.

**Status:** a future concept, not a current product.
</div>
</FoundGrid>

<PlainWords label="In plain words">
a chat assistant can be brilliant at the jobs it was trained for and still be <Highlight>narrow</Highlight>. AGI would <Highlight>learn a new intellectual job</Highlight> the way a person can move from maths to writing to planning. That is a <Highlight>research goal</Highlight>. It is not a box you can tick on a product page.
</PlainWords>

### AGI in more detail

<TwoCol>
<div>
### What would make intelligence general?

- ✓ Move a skill from one domain to a very different one.
- ✓ Handle unfamiliar tasks without task-specific retraining.
- ✓ Use language, perception, memory, tools, and reasoning together.
- ✓ Recover from mistakes over a long piece of work.
- ✓ Stay reliable outside the examples used in a demo.
</div>
<div>
### No single agreed finish line

AGI has no universally accepted scientific definition or pass/fail test. OpenAI's Charter describes it as "highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work" — that definition belongs to OpenAI; it is not a universal standard.

A model can beat people at coding, exams, or games and still fail at reliability, the physical world, novel situations, or sustained work. **A strong benchmark is not proof of AGI.**
</div>
</TwoCol>

### How to discuss AGI claims responsibly

| Ask | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| What definition is being used? | "AGI" can mean human-level cognition, economic performance, autonomy, or something else. |
| Which tasks and conditions were tested? | A narrow benchmark may not measure transfer, robustness, or real-world work. |
| How much human help, tuning, and compute were required? | The complete system may be more capable than the base model, but its autonomy is less than the headline suggests. |
| Does it remain safe and reliable? | Capability without controllability, safety, and predictable behavior is not dependable general intelligence. |

<NoteBar>
<strong>Current status — 13 October 2026:</strong> frontier systems are more multimodal and more able to use tools, but AGI remains a disputed goal rather than a settled, independently verified milestone.
</NoteBar>

### Based on functionality

<PlotFunctionalityStrip />

| Type | Description | Example / status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reactive machines** | React only to current input; <Highlight>no memory</Highlight>. | IBM Deep Blue. |
| **Limited memory** | Use past data or retained context to make decisions. | Many current AI systems. |
| **Theory of mind** | Would robustly model other agents' emotions, beliefs, and intentions. | Hypothetical, no accepted evidence — research stage. |
| **Self-aware AI** | Would possess consciousness and self-awareness. | Hypothetical. |

> **Taxonomy note:** these capability and functionality labels are popular teaching frameworks, not universally accepted scientific stages or a guaranteed path of AI development.
